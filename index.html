Debate

For student and early-career investors looking at one company. When a stock moves, what you can usually find out is that there was an earnings miss, rather than which arguments about the company are still unsettled and still affecting the price.

The problem

What I do now is look at the days a stock moved most and read the news from those days. Usually it tells me there was an earnings miss, which doesn't explain very much. The thing that actually mattered might be something like the useful life a company assumes for its GPUs being shorter than the market had expected.

That kind of detail doesn't appear in headlines. It comes up in analyst questions on earnings calls, and in the wording of 10-K risk factors, so those are what the product reads.

What makes a debate worth showing

  • Specific. “GPU useful-life assumptions,” not “margin pressure.”
  • Unresolved. Not priced in 90–100% in one direction.
  • Long-running. A debate from years ago can still weigh on the price.

It should work in both directions. From a company, find the debates about it. From a debate, find the companies it affects most. The second one is the half I want for myself: I usually know what the argument is before I know which companies it actually lands on.

Focus: AI

The app calls a model itself, with tools, and I check how well it works.

I picked AI because the output is the product. If a debate comes back vague, or turns out to be something everyone already agrees on, no other part of the app can tell me that. I have built sign-in, a database and external data sources into earlier assignments. I have not written prompts, given a model tools, or tried to measure whether its answers are any good.

In practice that means the agent decides which sources to pull rather than following a fixed order, and what comes back is structured: a bull claim, a bear claim, the quotes behind each one, and how much the model thinks is already priced in. Because it is structured I can score it against a rubric for being specific, two-sided and material, across about 20 outputs.

The priced-in figure is the model's opinion rather than a measurement, and it is shown with its reasoning. It is also the one part the rubric cannot check.

Users

My dad — a senior investment professional

When a stock he follows moves and he doesn't know why, he calls the analyst and the trading desk, and if there's no clear reason he puts it down to trading flow. To defend a position, his team writes a memo, builds a model with a few scenarios and sets out what they think the market is missing. He reads filings himself. What he complained about was that some data, like segment margins, is hard to find because companies don't break it out clearly, and that sell-side reports tend to repeat each other.

My brother — a casual trader

Gets his information from Reddit, Twitter, podcasts and YouTube, usually without checking whether it is accurate, and has bought more of something because an argument sounded reasonable. He doesn't read earnings calls or filings, only summaries. What he said he couldn't find was a source that explains why a stock moved without being overly positive or negative.

My roommate — a quant trader

Most of these questions didn't apply to him. He works with signals, risk limits and backtests, and his firm processes transcripts and filings automatically rather than having people read them. He distrusts arguments that sound convincing, because he assumes they are already reflected in the price. He said his trading is detached from fundamentals, and that it would be interesting to understand the names he trades more personally.

Roadmap

Nov 10–13The model and its tools

Give the agent its sources — filings and earnings calls — and work on the prompt until the debates it returns are specific and two-sided rather than vague.

Nov 13–17v1

The look-up itself. Type a company, get its debates back with both sides and the quotes behind them, and sign in to use it. Score twenty outputs to see whether the answers are any good. Due on the 17th.

Nov 17–24The interface

Build out the real thing. Debates you can read through rather than a list, both sides against each other, the evidence underneath, and what the stock did on the days each debate was in the news. Thanksgiving week, so work I can do in short stretches.

Nov 24–28The second direction

Describe a view in your own words — that GPU useful life is too aggressive — and get back the companies most exposed to it, ranked, with the same evidence. It finds the candidates by searching the full text of SEC filings for the language a company would use, then reads the ones that come back.

Nov 28–Dec 1The fair

Both directions working: a company to the debates about it, and a view to the companies it touches, for any company that files with the SEC.

In v1, Nov 17

  • Look up any public company. A dozen or so are kept ready so the first one is instant.
  • Three to five debates for each one, in order of how much they seem to matter.
  • Both sides of every debate, with the quote it came from and a link to the filing or call.
  • How much of it the model thinks is already priced in, and why it says so.
  • The days each debate was in the news, what the stock did on each, and how many days that is.
  • Sign in.
  • My own scoring of 20 outputs, to see whether the answers are any good.

In v2, at the fair

  • Describe a view in plain English and get the companies most exposed to it, ranked.
  • How long each debate has been running, taken from five years of filings.
  • A chart of the stock around debate days, instead of a list of dates.
  • Any company that files with the SEC, in either direction.

Vercel
Hosting, free Hobby plan, same as the earlier assignments.
OpenAI API
Model calls and tool calling, on credits I already have. I need structured output, which a chat window doesn't give me.
SEC EDGAR
Free, 10 requests a second, every public company. Risk factors and management commentary go back years. Its full-text search also returns which companies' filings match a phrase, which is how a view gets turned into a list of companies.
Equibles
Earnings call transcripts, 100 a day on the free tier. The analyst questions are where arguments get stated plainly. The cap means transcripts are a bonus rather than a requirement: a company whose call isn't cached yet is still read from its filings.
Alpaca
Daily prices, free, every US listing back to 2016. Alpha Vantage's free key only gives 100 days, unadjusted, which gets returns wrong around a stock split.
